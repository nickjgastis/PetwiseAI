It's the right question to ask. Your medical records are a legal document, a communication tool for your team and a history of every patient you've seen. Before you let software help write them, you should know what you're signing up for.

Here are honest answers to the questions we hear most.

## Will the AI get things wrong?

Sometimes, yes. Any AI can mishear a word or misread context, the same way a human scribe or a tired technician can.

That's why the workflow matters. With PetWise, the AI writes a draft and you review it before it goes anywhere. Every section is editable. Nothing is added to your PIMS until you copy it there.

Think of it like a capable new assistant: fast and thorough, but you still check their work. In practice, most vets find the AI catches details they would have forgotten by the end of the day, not the other way around.

## Who is responsible for the record?

You are, just like today. AI doesn't change that. It changes how long it takes you to get there.

The upside is that records written right after the appointment, while it's fresh, tend to be more complete than notes written from memory at 9pm.

## What happens to my data?

Fair question for any software you use. Here's how PetWise approaches it:

- **We don't sell your data.** Ever.
- **Records are tied to your account** and protected behind secure sign in.
- **You stay in control.** Edit, save or delete records as you see fit.

Our [privacy policy](/privacy) explains exactly how data is stored and used.

## Should I enter client personal information?

No, and you don't need to. PetWise is built for patient medical information: history, findings, assessment and plan. Leave out client names, phone numbers and addresses. Your PIMS already holds that.

## Is AI clinical advice safe to use?

Tools like PetQuery are great for quick references: dose ranges, differential lists, protocol refreshers and plain language client explanations. They're a starting point, not a replacement for your clinical judgment.

Treat AI answers the way you'd treat a colleague's quick suggestion in the hallway. Useful, often right and always worth a sanity check against your own experience.

## The bigger risk

Here's a different way to look at it. The real risk in most clinics isn't AI. It's burnout, rushed records and notes written hours after the fact.

When documentation takes minutes instead of hours, records get better and vets get their evenings back.
